AfriSalaries

Salary-band classifier across 8 African markets

Status: Deployed (May 2026)  |  Last updated: 15 Nov 2026

Problem

Over 70% of African tech job posts hide salary information, so candidates cannot benchmark an offer. AfriSalaries takes the text of a job description and a country, and returns a salary band (LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH relative to the local market) instead of an exact figure.

Requirements

  • Predict a salary band for a tech role from job-description text and a country.
  • Cover 8 African markets.
  • Return a salary range and the factors behind the band.

Data

1,526 real rows from the Stack Overflow Annual Developer Survey 2022 to 2025 (CC BY-SA 4.0), reduced from 4,911 African responses: rows with missing or unusable salaries were removed, currencies were converted with year-specific rates, salaries were kept between $3,000 and $200,000, duplicates were removed and South Africa was capped at three times Nigeria. No synthetic rows.

Coverage is uneven: South Africa 807 rows, Nigeria 269, Kenya 225, and five markets with 28 to 61 rows each.

Architecture

Implementation

  • XGBoost classifier on 3,000 TF-IDF features, 8 country columns and 8 numerical features.
  • Salary ranges come from per-band, per-country medians. A per-band regressor gave negative R-squared, so I did not use one.
  • Two exact-salary regression designs were rejected on validation (R-squared 0.3388, then 0.3846; MAPE 92.1% and 87.8%), so I moved to bands. I documented both.

Evaluation

Test accuracy: 59.5%. This is the share of held-out test rows whose predicted band matched the actual band. The three bands are salary tertiles (below $17,143, $17,143 to $47,872, above $47,872 per year), so chance is about 33.3%. Five-fold cross-validation accuracy is 56.2%. Precision is 0.72 for HIGH and 0.51 for MEDIUM.

Production engineering

A FastAPI service on Render serves the model to a React frontend on Vercel. Model files are backed up outside the repository.

My contribution

I built the data pipeline, models, API and frontend, and deployed them as the sole developer.

Results and limitations

  • 59.5% accuracy is a moderate result, and I report it as such.
  • The data are self-reported survey answers from Stack Overflow users, who skew experienced and English-speaking. The README warns of an upward bias.
  • Five markets have fewer than 100 rows, so estimates there are indicative only.
  • The model predicts base salary only, and it is not a legal benchmark.

What I would do next

  • Add real job-board data (BrighterMonday and Fuzu), aiming for 5,000 or more rows.
  • Return to exact-salary regression once there are more than 3,000 rows per country.
  • Add prediction history and a feedback loop in PostgreSQL.
  • Restore SHAP explanations once regression is viable.

Reproducibility and links

The live app is the working demonstration. Source and setup steps are in the repository README. Data: Stack Overflow Annual Developer Survey, CC BY-SA 4.0.
