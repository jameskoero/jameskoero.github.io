How the flood model was evaluated

Nyando Flood AI: protocol first, sealed events, and what did not hold

Case studiesResearchWritingExperienceSkillsAboutCV

Last updated: 18 Nov 2026

Result

The registered model beats elevation alone by +0.0384 mean per-event AUC, pooled over 50 events on the mappable frame (97.5% interval +0.0193 to +0.0611; 36 wins, 9 losses). Three other claims did not hold and are reported as such.

Principles

  • Test on unseen floods. Leave-one-event-out validation: each flood event is held out whole, and locations shared between events are removed.
  • Rules before results. Metrics, intervals and decision rules are written in a protocol and merged before any score exists.
  • Score sealed data once. 27 new flood dates (Block A) were built and scored a single time. Training rows at those locations were dropped first.
  • Compare with a simple baseline. Every model is compared with elevation alone, paired by event: 10,000 bootstrap resamples of events, 97.5% interval.

The mappable frame is the protocol's primary analysis. It excludes points inside the flood product's exclusion mask, where no label can be trusted.

What did not hold

  • The logistic champion's gain over elevation alone was not shown on the sealed events: +0.0117, 97.5% interval −0.0107 to +0.0381 (11 wins, 16 losses).
  • No advantage was shown under a 1 km spatial buffer or an out-of-time split.
  • A physics-constrained neural network was not promoted (details).

Leakage audit

Land cover had the largest effect on the model, which can be a sign of leakage. A separate audit with its own protocol found that, on the sealed events, land cover does not separate floods from non-floods within the same land-cover class. Its contribution comes from how classes differ in the sample. Where clay content is observed, its value does add discrimination. The model card states both.

The earlier result I retracted

The first version reported AUC 0.9717, F1 0.9022 and cross-validated AUC 0.9727. The training file had only 2 flood-labelled rows out of 2,308, and SMOTE oversampling was applied before the train/test split, so the test set held copies of training points. I retracted the figures, added an erratum to both earlier Zenodo records on 3 November 2026, and rebuilt the data from independent Copernicus GFM flood labels.

Limits

  • Scores rank locations within a case-control sample. They are not flood probabilities.
  • Early-warning (temporal) skill is not shown; the evidence supports susceptibility ranking only.
