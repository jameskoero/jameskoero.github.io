Applied machine learning: Nyando Flood AI

Release v2.0.0, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23919053. Full write-up: case study.

Objective

Rank locations in five Nyando Basin wards by flood susceptibility, and test whether a more complex model beats elevation alone.

Method

Gradient boosting with monotonic constraints, compared with logistic regression, XGBoost and a physics-constrained neural network. The network violated the constraints and was not promoted.

Data and validation

4,420 real rows from 35 Sentinel-1 scene dates, with Copernicus GFM flood labels and Google Earth Engine features. No synthetic data. Leave-one-event-out mean per-event AUC 0.943 on 23 events.

Experimental design

Protocol and decision rules written before scoring. A sealed set of 27 events (D20) scored once. Training rows at sealed-event locations removed before fitting.

Baselines

Elevation only, and a registered logistic champion.

The mappable frame is the protocol's primary analysis.

The registered model's gain over elevation alone is +0.0384 mean AUC, pooled over 50 events (95% interval +0.0193 to +0.0611; 36 wins, 9 losses).

Deployment and ONNX validation

Custom ONNX exporter with numerical parity of 3.3e-16. The FastAPI service verifies the model file's SHA-256 before loading it. Hash-locked Docker image.

Limitations

  • The champion's gain over elevation alone is not established: its interval includes zero on both frames.
  • No advantage was shown under a 1 km spatial buffer or an out-of-time split.
  • Scores rank locations. They are not calibrated probabilities.

Future work

  • Test for early-warning (temporal) skill. The current evidence supports susceptibility ranking only.
  • A further block of 26 events is held sealed for a future challenger.

Correction

An earlier version reported AUC 0.9717, F1 0.9022 and cross-validation 0.9727 on 2,308 rows. A leakage audit showed the headline metric was circular. I retracted it and published an erratum on Zenodo.
